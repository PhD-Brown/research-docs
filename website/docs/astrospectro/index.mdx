## Scientific purpose

AstroSpectro exists to investigate a deceptively simple question: when a machine learning
classifier is trained on a large stellar spectroscopic survey, *which physical indicators
actually determine its decisions?*

The classical MK spectral classification system places effective temperature — traced by
Balmer series strength — at the centre of the A/F/G/K/M hierarchy.
Modern surveys like LAMOST DR5 now make it possible to train classifiers on hundreds of
thousands of spectra and then interrogate those models via post-hoc interpretability
methods. AstroSpectro does exactly this, with four explicit scientific goals:

- **Classify** stellar spectra into five Harvard types (A, F, G, K, M) with calibrated
  confidence, using only features derivable from the spectrum itself.
- **Extract** physically meaningful descriptors that encode astrophysical observables —
  line strengths, profile shapes, molecular band indices — rather than raw flux pixels.
- **Validate** what the model learns against independent stellar parameters from Gaia DR3,
  confirming or challenging the astrophysical coherence of the classification structure.
- **Avoid** observational shortcuts: positional, temporal, and survey-specific metadata
  are excluded at the architectural level via `spectro_only = True`.

## Three-tier architecture

The project is organised in three tiers that build on each other and can be read independently.

<div className="summary-cards">

<div className="summary-card accent-blue">

**Tier 1 — Core pipeline**
*Preprocessing → Feature engineering → Classification*

The foundational tier: from raw FITS spectra to a trained, calibrated classifier.
It covers the `SmartDownloader`, the `FeatureEngineer` (183 descriptors), the
`GaiaCrossmatcher` (Gaia DR3 positional match), and the `SpectralClassifier`
(XGBoost, isotonic calibration, SHAP output).
`spectro_only = True` is the central design constraint of this tier.

See: [Core Pipeline](./astrospectro/pipeline)

</div>

<div className="summary-card accent-amber">

**Tier 2 — Dimensionality reduction**
*PCA · UMAP · t-SNE · Autoencoder · HDBSCAN*

An independent multi-method exploration of the 183-feature matrix in low dimensions.
This tier covers the algorithmic foundations of each method, their hyperparameter
sensitivity, their Procrustes stability, and the physical interpretation of their axes
and clusters. It constitutes the analytical core of the PHY-3500 course project
(Baker, Caillat & Jean, Université Laval, 2026).

See: [Dimensionality Reduction](./astrospectro/dimred)

</div>

<div className="summary-card accent-green">

**Tier 3 — Scientific validation**
*SHAP · Physical cross-checks · Claims register*

Post-hoc interpretability and astrophysical validation of the classifier's decisions.
This tier contains the SHAP global importance analysis, per-class beeswarm plots,
feature interaction matrices, physical cross-validation against Gaia DR3 parameters,
and the Scientific Claims Register that tracks every finding with its evidence level.

See: [Scientific Analysis](./astrospectro/science)

</div>

</div>
