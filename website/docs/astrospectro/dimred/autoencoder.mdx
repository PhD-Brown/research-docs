# Autoencoder — Non-linear Dimensionality Reduction

This page documents the results of the `SpectralAutoencoder` neural autoencoder applied to the **183 spectroscopic features** of N = 43,019 LAMOST DR5 × Gaia DR3 spectra. The autoencoder is the third dimensionality reduction method studied in the PHY-3500 project, complementing PCA (linear) and UMAP/t-SNE (non-parametric). Its role is to provide a **non-linear parametric representation**: it learns a compression and can project new spectra without full recomputation.

:::note[PHY-3500 article reference]
The autoencoder is mentioned in the Baker, Caillat & Jean article (28 April 2026) but its detailed results are not developed in the main text — the reader is referred to this documentation page. Key results: MSE(AE, z=2) = 0.489 ≈ PCA(k=10), and the discovery that latent axis 1 encodes **gravity** rather than temperature.
:::

## Why an autoencoder?

Previous methods have complementary limitations:

| Method | Linear? | Parametric? | Main advantage | Limitation |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|:---|
| **PCA** | ✓ | ✓ | Interpretable loadings · reproducible | Blind to non-linear relationships |
| **UMAP / t-SNE** | ✗ | Partial | Topological structure · clusters | No exact `transform()` for new points |
| **Autoencoder** | ✗ | ✓ | Non-linear + parametric | Black box · training cost |

The autoencoder is **non-linear** (it can capture structures that PCA flattens) and **parametric** (once trained, encoding a new spectrum is a simple forward pass). This is the ideal combination for a production pipeline.

## Architecture

<AEArchitecture />

### Justification of architecture choices

**Symmetric MLP (183 → 256 → 128 → 64 → 2 → 64 → 128 → 256 → 183):**
The initial expansion to 256 (> 183) is not an error — it allows the network to create richer intermediate representations before compression. This is common practice for autoencoders on spectral data (Portillo et al. 2020, AJ 160:45).

**BatchNorm + Dropout(p=0.1):**
BatchNorm stabilises gradients and accelerates convergence. Light dropout (0.1) avoids overfitting without degrading reconstruction — stellar spectra are regular enough that stronger dropout is counterproductive.

**Latent dimension z = 2:**
Deliberate choice for 2D visualisation and fair comparison with PCA(k=2) and UMAP(2D). A z = 8 or 16 would achieve much lower MSE but would lose visual readability.

```python
from src.dimred.autoencoder import SpectralAutoencoder

# Training
ae = SpectralAutoencoder(input_dim=183, latent_dim=2, hidden_dims=[256, 128, 64])
history = ae.fit(X_train_scaled, epochs=200, lr=1e-3, batch_size=512, patience=15)

# Encode 43,019 spectra → 2D latent space
Z_ae = ae.encode(X_scaled)   # shape: (43019, 2)

# Reconstruction
X_recon = ae.decode(Z_ae)    # shape: (43019, 183)
mse = ae.reconstruction_mse(X_scaled)  # → 0.489
```

| **STAR** | 42,956 | 0.485 | **0.227** | 0.974 | 1× (reference) |

| **QSO** | 7 | 5.794 | **0.954** | 18.637 | **~107× (median)** |

:::tip[Unsupervised anomaly detection]
QSOs (*quasi-stellar objects*) have a reconstruction error 107× higher than stars (median). The autoencoder, trained almost exclusively on stars (99.85% of the sample), has never learned the spectral structure of QSOs — their broad emission lines (Ly-α, CIV) are radically different. This result demonstrates that **reconstruction error can serve as an anomaly score**: a spectrum with MSE >> 1 is likely non-stellar.
:::

### Latent space organisation

The latent space grid (`ae_latent_grid.png`) reveals a physically coherent organisation:

- **Axis 2** (thermal, ρ = +0.793 with Teff) deploys the M-K-G-F-A sequence from bottom to top
- **Axis 1** (gravity, ρ = +0.340 with log g) separates dwarfs and giants
- Galaxies and QSOs are pushed far from the main stellar mass — their high reconstruction error corresponds to their distance in latent space

This result is an **independent confirmation** of the HDBSCAN discovery: the dwarf/giant distinction is a genuine physical dimension of spectral space.

## Latent space interpolation

The parametric autoencoder allows linear interpolation between two points in latent space and decoding intermediate positions into physical spectra:

```python
# Interpolation between a cool star (K) and a hot star (A)
# idx_cold: Teff = 4,200 K · idx_hot: Teff = 7,984 K
# Latent distance = 11.44 units

t = np.linspace(0, 1, 10)
Z_interp = np.outer(1-t, Z_ae[idx_cold]) + np.outer(t, Z_ae[idx_hot])
X_interp = ae.decode(Z_interp)
# → 10 synthetic intermediate spectra between 4,200 and 7,984 K
```

The interpolation produces a **continuous and physically plausible transition**: Balmer lines progressively weaken and Ca II lines strengthen. The latent distance of 11.44 units for a Teff variation of ~3,800 K confirms that axis 2 linearly encodes temperature.

## Latent arithmetic — Stellar analogies

Following the word2vec idea (Mikolov et al. 2013), does the latent space support analogies of the type `K_giant − K_dwarf + G_dwarf ≈ G_giant`?

```python
# K_giant − K_dwarf + G_dwarf = ?
z_pred  = z_K_giant - z_K_dwarf + z_G_dwarf
z_target = z_G_giant

distance = np.linalg.norm(z_pred - z_target)
# → distance = 2.1 (= ~18% of total range) — FUNCTIONAL ANALOGY
```

The result indicates that the latent space encodes **the dwarf↔giant direction consistently** across spectral types — suggesting the network has learned a representation that partially factorises (temperature, gravity) in a measurable way.
