# Dimensionality Reduction — PCA, UMAP, t-SNE

This page documents the comparison of three dimensionality reduction methods applied to the **183 spectroscopic features** of N = 43,019 LAMOST DR5 × Gaia DR3 spectra. It covers the algorithmic foundations of each method, their hyperparameters, quantitative results and stability comparison across independent runs.

:::info[Central question]
To what extent are the **linear** (PCA), **topological** (UMAP) and **probabilistic** (t-SNE) approaches equivalent at capturing stellar physical information when projecting from 183 dimensions to 2? What are the numerical and physical trade-offs?
:::

## 1. PCA — Principal Component Analysis

### Principle and algorithm

PCA seeks the directions $\mathbf{w}_k$ that maximise projected variance. For $N = 43{,}019$ and $p = 183$, the truncated SVD is computed by the **Lanczos algorithm** (Krylov method, complexity $\mathcal{O}(Kp^2 + KNp)$). Beyond a size threshold, scikit-learn automatically activates **randomised SVD** (Halko et al., 2011), reducing complexity to $\mathcal{O}(NpK)$ with controlled error.

The **Eckart-Young theorem** guarantees the optimality of the truncation: the K-component reconstruction is the best rank-K approximation of $\mathbf{X}$ in the Frobenius norm sense.

### Hyperparameters used

```python
from sklearn.decomposition import PCA

pca = PCA(n_components=91, random_state=42)   # 95% variance threshold
Z_pca = pca.fit_transform(X_scaled)           # (43019, 91)
```

The choice $K = 91$ is not arbitrary: it is the number of components needed to capture **95% of total variance** of the 183 features.

### Quantitative results

- **PC1**: 16.9% variance ($\lambda_1 = 30.19$), thermal axis, ρ(Teff) = **+0.831**
- **PC2**: 12.0% variance ($\lambda_2 = 21.39$), metallicity/SNR axis
- **Cumulative PC1+PC2**: 28.8% — two components capture only ~30% of information
- **95% variance** requires K = **91 components**

For the complete axis interpretation (loadings, Gaia correlations, eigenspectra), see [Physical interpretation of PCA](./pca-physical-interpretation).

## 2. UMAP — Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection

### Principle

UMAP (McInnes, Healy & Melville, 2018) is based on the assumption that data is distributed on a low-dimensional Riemannian manifold. The algorithm builds a fuzzy representation of local topology in the original space, then optimises a low-dimensional representation that best preserves this topology.

### Fuzzy graph construction

For each point $\mathbf{x}_i$, let $\rho_i$ be the distance to its nearest neighbour and $\sigma_i > 0$ a smoothing parameter. The weight of connection $i \to j$ is:

$$
w_{ij} = \exp\left(-\frac{\max(0, d(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_j) - \rho_i)}{\sigma_i}\right)
$$

The $\rho_i$ term guarantees a unit connection to the nearest neighbour. Weights are made symmetric via the fuzzy union: $\bar{w}_{ij} = w_{ij} + w_{ji} - w_{ij} \cdot w_{ji}$.

k-NN graph construction via random projection trees (RP trees, Dasgupta & Freund, 2008) runs in $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$.

### Low-dimensional optimisation

Weights in 2D are modelled by a Cauchy family curve:

$$
\tilde{w}_{ij}(\mathbf{y}) = \frac{1}{1 + a\|\mathbf{y}_i - \mathbf{y}_j\|^{2b}}
$$

where $a, b$ are adjusted from `min_dist`. The cross-entropy divergence is minimised by **stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with negative sampling**.

### Hyperparameters used

```python
import umap

reducer = umap.UMAP(
    n_components=2,
    n_neighbors=15,       # local/global trade-off — optimal in [15, 50]
    min_dist=0.1,         # cluster compactness
    n_epochs=200,
    metric='euclidean',
    random_state=42,
)
Z_umap = reducer.fit_transform(Z_pca)   # input: 91 PCA components
```

**Computation time:** 40.1 s (Ryzen 9 5950X, 32 threads)

### Results: "continuous continent" structure

UMAP produces a **"continuous continent" structure** — opposite to the t-SNE "archipelago". The Harvard sequence (M-K-G-F-A) unfolds continuously; hot stars (A-F types) form a "peninsula" in the upper-left corner of the projection.

## 3. t-SNE — t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding

### Principle

t-SNE (van der Maaten & Hinton, 2008) models similarities as probabilities in both spaces. In the original space, a Gaussian kernel centred on each point gives symmetric joint probabilities $p_{ij}$ computed once.

### Crowding problem and Student-t distribution

In high dimensions, the many neighbours at moderate distance cannot be accommodated in a 2D plane with a Gaussian distribution. The Student-t distribution with 1 degree of freedom (Cauchy) provides higher probability in 2D for large distances, solving the *crowding problem*:

$$
q_{ij} = \frac{\left(1 + \|\mathbf{y}_i - \mathbf{y}_j\|^2\right)^{-1}}{\sum_{k \neq l}\left(1 + \|\mathbf{y}_k - \mathbf{y}_l\|^2\right)^{-1}}
$$

The Kullback-Leibler objective $\text{KL}(P \| Q) = \sum_{i \neq j} p_{ij} \ln(p_{ij}/q_{ij})$ is minimised by gradient descent. The Barnes-Hut approximation (van der Maaten, 2014) reduces complexity from $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$.

### Hyperparameters used

```python
from sklearn.manifold import TSNE

tsne = TSNE(
    n_components=2,
    perplexity=30,          # effective number of neighbours — optimal in [15, 50]
    init='pca',             # PCA initialisation — key for reproducibility
    n_iter=1000,
    random_state=42,
)
Z_tsne = tsne.fit_transform(Z_pca)   # input: 91 PCA components
```

**Computation time:** 80.2 s (i.e., 2× UMAP, Ryzen 9 5950X)

t-SNE is **entirely non-parametric**: coordinates $\mathbf{y}_i$ do not generalise out-of-sample.

### Results: "archipelago" structure

t-SNE produces a markedly different geometry from UMAP. Rather than a continuous manifold, t-SNE produces an **"archipelago" of compact, well-separated masses**:

- Hot stars (Teff > 7,000 K, A-F types) form a distinct cluster at the top of the projection
- The large central mass groups K and G main-sequence stars (4,500–6,500 K)
- Log g colouring reveals a tendency to isolate subgiants (log g ~ 3–3.5) in discrete clusters, where UMAP produces a continuous transition

**Fundamental UMAP vs t-SNE difference:** UMAP better preserves continuous variations (readable Teff and [Fe/H] gradients), while t-SNE excels at sharp separation of discrete sub-populations. t-SNE compresses gradual variations in favour of local discontinuities.

## 4. Stability comparison — Procrustes

### Procrustes metric

Two independent projections $Z_1, Z_2$ (different random seeds) are compared after optimal alignment (rotation, reflection, scaling):

$$
d_P = \min_{\Omega \in \mathcal{O}(2), c > 0} \|Z_1 - cZ_2\Omega\|_F^2
$$

$d_P = 0$ indicates projections identical up to an isometric transformation.

### Explanation of the counter-intuitive result

This result is counter-intuitive: both methods minimise a non-convex objective by stochastic gradient. The explanation lies in two factors:

1. **PCA initialisation of t-SNE** (`init='pca'`): fixes an identical starting position across runs, regardless of the random seed.
2. **KL penalty structure**: concentrates energy on immediate neighbours — makes optimisation insensitive to the seed for large structures.

UMAP uses a spectral initialisation (Laplacian decomposition) whose result can vary between seeds, and its SGD with negative sampling introduces more marked stochasticity in repulsive forces.

:::caution[Inter-cluster distances in UMAP]
The increased stability of t-SNE does not mean t-SNE is "better". Distances *between* clusters in t-SNE are not interpretable — the KL objective only penalises local discrepancies. UMAP offers a better balance between local and global structure, at the cost of greater variability between runs.
:::

## 5. Evaluation metrics

### Neighbourhood fidelity — Trustworthiness T(k)

The Venna & Kaski (2006) statistic measures what proportion of the k nearest neighbours in the projection were effectively close in the original space:

$$
T(k) = 1 - \frac{2}{Nk(2N-3k-1)} \sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j \in U_k(i)} (r(i,j) - k)
$$

where $U_k(i)$ groups the k projected neighbours that were not k-neighbours in the original space. $T(k) = 1$ is perfect fidelity.

```python
from sklearn.manifold import trustworthiness

T_umap = trustworthiness(X_scaled, Z_umap, n_neighbors=15)
T_tsne = trustworthiness(X_scaled, Z_tsne, n_neighbors=15)
```

**Figure reference:** `umap_trustworthiness.png`

### Explained variance and MSE (PCA only)

$$
V(K) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_k}{\sum_{j}\lambda_j}, \quad \text{MSE}(K) = \frac{1}{p}\left(1 - V(K)\right)
$$

| Threshold V(K) | K required | Corresponding MSE |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 80% | 51 | 0.200 |
| 90% | 73 | 0.100 |
| **95%** | **91** | **0.050** |
| 99% | 100 | 0.010 |
| K = 2 | — | 0.696 |

## 6. Study limitations

**Class imbalance.** The dataset is 99.85% dominated by stars. The 63 galaxies and QSOs are too rare to evaluate their behaviour in projections and were not included in the HDBSCAN analysis.

**PCA pre-reduction.** UMAP and t-SNE operate on the 91 PCA components, not the 183 raw features. Any small-scale non-linearity already eliminated by linear truncation is permanently lost — an inherent bias in the pipeline.

**No objective criterion for hyperparameters.** For UMAP and t-SNE, no theoretical criterion exists for choosing `n_neighbors` or perplexity. Sensitivity analyses show that qualitative structure is robust for $k \in [15, 50]$ and perplexity $\in [15, 50]$, but fine topology varies. PCA, in contrast, has an objective, reproducible truncation criterion (variance threshold or eigenspectrum elbow).

**Intrinsic dimensionality.** The 91 components needed for 95% variance indicate that the 2D manifold hypothesis is approximate. The 2D projection is necessarily distorted; the emergence of the Harvard sequence in these two dimensions constitutes a non-trivial astrophysical result *precisely because* the compression is extreme.
