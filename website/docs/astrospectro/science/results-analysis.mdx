# Results Analysis

This page presents the results obtained by the AstroSpectro pipeline on N = 43,019 LAMOST DR5 × Gaia DR3 spectra described by p = 183 spectroscopic features. Results cover two complementary axes: unsupervised analysis through dimensionality reduction (PCA, UMAP, t-SNE, HDBSCAN) and supervised classification (XGBoost with SHAP validation).

## Axis 1 — Unsupervised dimensionality reduction

### PCA — The linear baseline

PCA on the 183 standardised features reveals **high intrinsic dimensionality**: 91 components are needed to capture 95% of variance, with no clear elbow in the eigenvalue spectrum.

| Threshold V(K) | Components K | Interpretation |
|:---:|:---:|:---|
| 28.8% | 2 | PC1 + PC2 only — visualisation |
| 80% | 51 | Fast analysis |
| **95%** | **91** | **Retained threshold for UMAP/t-SNE** |
| 99% | 100 | Near-total variance |

<PCAVarianceMilestones />

**PC1 (16.9%, λ₁ = 30.19) — Thermal axis:** ρ(PC1, Teff) = +0.831. Dominated by Balmer lines (31.5%) and ionised calcium (22.4%). Hot stars (A-F, > 7,000 K) occupy the positive side; cool stars (K-M, < 5,000 K) the negative side.

**PC2 (12.0%, λ₂ = 21.39) — Metallicity axis (partially contaminated):** Dominated by iron and metals (43.3%). The correlation ρ(PC2, SNRr) = −0.310 reveals instrumental contamination — PC2 is not a pure metallicity axis.

| K components | MSE(K) | Variance captured |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2 | 0.696 | 30.4% |
| 10 | 0.494 | 50.6% |
| 50 | 0.196 | 80.4% |
| **91** | **~0.050** | **95.0%** |

**Eigenspectra on raw flux (10,000 spectra, 3,921 channels):** Three components are sufficient to explain 91.3% of raw flux variance (vs 91 components for the 183 features). PC1_flux = colour slope (Teff) · PC2_flux = infrared rise cool stars · PC3_flux = blue/red instrumental cut.

For the complete interpretation, see [Physical interpretation of PCA](./pca-physical-interpretation).

### UMAP — Topological structure and HDBSCAN clusters

UMAP is applied to the 91 PCA components with `n_neighbors=15`, `min_dist=0.1`, 200 epochs (t = 40.1 s, Ryzen 9 5950X). The projection reveals a **"continuous continent" structure** where the Harvard sequence (M-K-G-F-A) unfolds without supervision.

**Correlations:** ρ(UMAP axis 1, Teff) = +0.464. The luminosity class (dwarfs vs giants) induces a visible topological bifurcation when coloured by log g.

**Negative control:** UMAP applied to data with randomly permuted columns → compact homogeneous cloud with no structure. The observed structure is of **physical origin**, not an algorithmic artefact.

#### HDBSCAN clusters — 20 groups without any label

<HDBSCANClusters />

:::tip[Original astrophysical result]
Without any label or log g information, UMAP organises spectra such that HDBSCAN **recovers the dwarf/subgiant distinction at fixed Teff**. Clusters C19, C1 and C12 are spectrally distinguished by strengthened Ca II lines and weakened Balmer lines — the signature of lower radiation pressure at giant surfaces.
:::

### t-SNE — Probabilistic structure and stability

t-SNE is applied to the same 91 PCA components with `perplexity=30`, `init='pca'`, 1,000 iterations (t = 80.2 s, i.e. 2× UMAP). The projection produces an **"archipelago" of compact separated masses**, opposite to the continuous UMAP structure.

- Hot stars (A-F, Teff > 7,000 K) form a distinct isolated cluster
- The central mass groups K-G main-sequence stars
- Log g colouring reveals partially isolated subgiant clusters

t-SNE excels at **sharp separation of discrete sub-populations**, while UMAP better preserves **continuous gradients** (Teff, [Fe/H]).

## Axis 3 — SHAP validation

### Central result

SHAP (*SHapley Additive exPlanations*) analysis on the `spectro_only=True` model reveals that **97.9% of the 30 most important features are physical spectral features**. This is the **central scientific validation** of the AstroSpectro pipeline: the model has learned to classify stars using exactly the same spectroscopic information that astrophysicists use manually.

## Limitations and next steps

**Class imbalance:** Galaxies (56) and QSOs (7) are too rare for HDBSCAN analysis and are not included in the 5-class classification.

**Intrinsic dimensionality:** The 91 components needed for 95% variance indicate that spectral diversity is genuinely high-dimensional. The 2D projection is an extreme compression (183 → 2), and the emergence of the Harvard sequence in these two dimensions is a non-trivial result.

**Next steps:**
- Characterisation of the 20 HDBSCAN clusters by cross-matching with rare object catalogues (variable stars, RGB giants, spectroscopic binaries)
- Large-scale Optuna optimisation on the ~139k available FITS files
- Anomaly analysis: intersection of HDBSCAN noise ∩ top 1% MSE autoencoder
- Extension to LAMOST DR10 (~10⁷ spectra) or SDSS
