I built a pipeline that models spectral classification based on the principles of stellar physics. The model autonomously learned that Calcium and Hydrogen line-strength ratios are the best temperature indicators, validating our feature engineering approach and allowing us to reach 56% accuracy.

Today, after several iterations of debugging, analysis, and optimization, we reached a significant milestone, increasing the model accuracy from 32% to a very respectable **56%**. Here is the story of that progression.

### Iteration 1: From Line Presence to Line Strength

The first improvement consisted in moving from a binary feature (is the line present?) to a continuous measurement: the **line strength**, approximated by the "prominence" of the absorption peak. This change had an immediate impact, raising our overall accuracy from 32% to **40%**. This step confirmed that the amount of physical information contained in the strength of a line is much richer than its mere presence.

### Iteration 2: The Power of Spectral Ratios

With a solid new baseline, we introduced even more sophisticated features: **strength ratios between different lines**. In astrophysics, these ratios are extremely powerful temperature indicators.

*   `feature_ratio_CaK_Hbeta = CaII K / Hβ`
*   `feature_ratio_Halpha_Hbeta = Hα / Hβ`

Adding just these two ratios once again pushed the model forward, reaching **50%** accuracy. The confusion matrix showed a clear improvement, but a feature-quality analysis revealed a critical weakness: the Hα line was missing in more than 50% of the spectra, making the ratios unreliable.

### Final Iteration: Tuning Through Visualization

At this stage, our visualization tools became crucial. The analysis showed that our peak-detection parameter (`prominence`) was too strict and ignored many real but shallower lines.

Thanks to our **interactive spectrum analyzer**, we were able to tune this parameter in real time. An optimized value of `prominence=0.23` was identified, allowing us to recover a large number of lines that had previously been ignored.

### The Final Result: 56% Accuracy

After rerunning the full pipeline with this optimized parameter on a new batch of **2607 spectra**, the results exceeded our expectations:

*   **Overall Accuracy: 56%**
*   **Feature Quality:** The missing-value problem was almost eliminated, with all base features present in more than **95%** of cases.
*   **Feature Relevance:** Feature importance is now much better balanced. `feature_Hβ` and the `ratio_CaK_Hbeta` stand out as the most powerful indicators, but all features now contribute meaningfully.

The final confusion matrix shows a much clearer diagonal, a sign of a more confident and more accurate model, with F1 scores above 0.54 for all main classes.

### Conclusion and Next Adventures

This research sprint was a striking demonstration of the power of the iterative method in data science. Starting from a simple model, and improving it step by step based on result analysis, we **improved our baseline by 24 percentage points**.

The AstroSpectro project now has a robust production pipeline and a suite of powerful analysis tools. The next exploration paths are already on the table:

*   **Enrich the features** to better classify cool stars (type M) by adding molecular bands.
*   **Experiment with more powerful models** such as XGBoost or neural networks.
*   **Move toward Deep Learning** by using the full spectrum as input to a convolutional neural network (CNN).

The laboratory is built. The tools are sharpened. The adventure continues! 🚀
